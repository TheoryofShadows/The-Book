# Launch posts

Written to be posted when the audio is live — not before. The audiobook is
the reason for someone to look today, and a launch without it spends the one
introduction each of these communities will give you.

Everything below states only what the site can be checked on. If a number
here stops being true, the post is wrong, not the site.

**Before posting anywhere:** the render has to be finished, uploaded, and
`data-audio="published"` set, and `tools/check_audio.py` has to pass. Until
then the site offers no recorded audio and every claim below about listening
is false.

## r/AcademicBiblical

The audience knows the field. They will be unimpressed by scale and very
interested in method, and they will check. Lead with what is unusual to
them: the ordering, the Ethiopian material, and the fact that the site
publishes what it is missing.

> **A free reader for 172 texts — canonical, apocryphal and pseudepigraphal — arranged by date of composition, now with a complete audio reading**
>
> I've been building this for a while and the audio finished rendering, so it
> seems a reasonable point to show it: https://thebookandme.com/
>
> What's in it: the Jewish, Protestant, Catholic and Eastern Orthodox canons
> complete; the Ethiopian Orthodox canon less four books that survive in no
> public-domain English translation; the pseudepigrapha, New Testament
> apocrypha and Apostolic Fathers. 172 works, 2,537 chapters, 1.22 million
> words. Arranged by when scholars believe each was written rather than by
> canonical order, which is the thing I actually wanted and couldn't find.
>
> Two parts I think are worth your scrutiny rather than mine:
>
> **It publishes its own accuracy audit.** The coverage table says the
> Ethiopian canon is at 90 of 94 units and names the four that are absent,
> with the reason (no public-domain English) and citations. It used to say
> "complete" while the underlying data said otherwise; there is now a test
> that fails the build if the front page overclaims against the data. Three
> books of Meqabyan and Josippon were missing from the table entirely — they
> are in it now, and the count got worse and true.
>
> **Five books were recovered from scans by word coordinates** rather than by
> OCR, and each says on its own page which witness it follows, what portion is
> printed, and what has not been checked about it.
>
> There are also 11 thematic threads that follow an idea across the corpus in
> composition order — where the afterlife arrives in the Hebrew Bible, what
> the New Testament is doing when it quotes 1 Enoch, and so on.
>
> The audio is a neural TTS reading of the whole library, rendered offline
> and served as one file per chapter with per-verse timings, so tapping a
> verse seeks to it exactly. It is not a human narrator and does not pretend
> to be. As far as I can tell several of these books have never had an
> English audio reading of any kind.
>
> Everything is public domain or openly licensed, there are no accounts, no
> tracking and no dependencies, and it works offline. Corrections very
> welcome — there is a published list of known problems and I would rather
> add to it than not know.

## Hacker News

Here the corpus is the hook but the engineering is the story. State the
constraints plainly; this audience rewards the specific and punishes the
grandiose.

**Title:** `The Book – 172 biblical and related texts in composition order, with a rendered audiobook`

> Author here. The site is a reader for the Jewish, Protestant, Catholic,
> Orthodox and (nearly all of) the Ethiopian canons plus the apocrypha and
> Apostolic Fathers — 172 works, 2,537 chapters, 1.22M words — ordered by
> date of composition rather than canonical order.
>
> The parts that might interest this crowd:
>
> **No dependencies.** No framework, no CDN, no build step for the client.
> The site is static HTML/CSS/JS generated by Python scripts and served from
> GitHub Pages. The reader is a single page behind hash routes, and because a
> fragment never reaches a server, the build also emits 2,724 real pages so
> the library is actually indexable.
>
> **The audiobook was precomputed because it could not be live.** Kokoro (82M
> params) runs at about 0.43x realtime in Chromium on one thread — WebAssembly
> gets no threads without COOP/COEP headers, which GitHub Pages cannot send.
> So the arithmetic is done once: ~100 hours of audio at ~3.7x realtime on a
> desktop, one Opus file per chapter with a JSON of per-verse offsets beside
> it. Seeking to a verse is then one assignment to currentTime and speed is
> playbackRate, which browsers time-stretch without shifting pitch. Each verse
> is synthesised separately so the offsets are exact rather than
> forced-aligned. It is 1.4GB, which is over the Pages artifact cap, so it
> lives on the Internet Archive.
>
> **The tests are mostly about not lying.** The build fails if the front page
> claims a canon is complete while the data says it is short; if the parsed
> chapter and verse counts drift from reference figures; if the map data goes
> over its size budget; or if the site offers a recorded reading while the
> archive item is missing. There is a published accuracy report listing what
> is wrong or absent, which is the part I would most like torn apart.
>
> Everything is public domain or openly licensed. No accounts, no tracking, no
> analytics. Works offline.

## Ethiopian Orthodox communities

Approach carefully and only if welcome. The right posture is offering
something and asking whether it is any good, not announcing. These are the
books the site covers that essentially nothing else does — and it is their
tradition, not yours.

Points worth making, briefly: the three books of Meqabyan and Josippon are
present; the coverage is 90 of 94 units with the four absent ones named and
the reason given; and the audio may be the first English reading of some of
them. Ask for correction rather than offering completeness.

## Notes on posting

**One at a time, a few days apart.** Simultaneous posting reads as a
campaign, and the feedback from the first will improve the second.

**Answer everything.** The accuracy report is the strongest thing here and it
only stays strong if corrections actually get made. A public list of known
problems is a promise.

**Do not claim it has never been done.** Neural TTS audiobooks are common and
Project Gutenberg has released tens of thousands. What is unusual is this
corpus, in this order, with per-verse sync, freely licensed. That claim is
defensible and does not need inflating — and this is a site whose whole
argument is that it would rather print a hole than a plausible sentence.
